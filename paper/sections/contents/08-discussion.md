## Discussion

The results compose into one account, and we state it with the causal commitments each level has actually earned. At the corpus level, documentation frequency predicts which concepts emerge, which regress, and where in the output ranking the correct continuation sits — a pre-registered, confound-controlled, cross-architecture correlation, and strictly a prediction, not a demonstrated cause. At the representation level, the knowledge that frequency gates is distributed and redundant: the attention structure that tracks emergence is real, general across domains, and causally inert, and the most concept-selective head in the network can be removed without consequence. At the decision level, failure is not absence — the correct continuation is present in the output distribution at every scale that has the knowledge, and what changes with scale is whether it wins the final election against higher-frequency alternatives, with the largest model's characteristic loss being not to a rival concept but to a degenerate self-reference loop.

We looked for wiring and found ink. The mechanistic program of this paper — head characterization, ablation, decomposition, decision-point analysis — kept returning the same verdict from different directions: the failure does not condense into a findable circuit object, because it is not object-shaped. Concept knowledge in this frequency regime behaves like a statistical property soaked into distributed weights, not like a mechanism grown in identifiable wiring. The circuit hunt came up empty because we were, correctly, looking for wiring where there was only ever ink. We consider the vacancy itself, documented with negative controls at three levels, to be this paper's mechanistic contribution.

Prior work established the macro-economics of this relationship: pretraining document frequency predicts factual recall, term frequency predicts few-shot reasoning on this same model suite, and scaling preferentially improves popular knowledge. This paper's contribution is the micro-physics — token-level rank evidence tied to per-concept scaling trajectories in a professional domain, with the frequency measured against the actual training corpus and every accuracy judgment deterministic and re-runnable. They counted documents; we watched elections.

One finding arrived by way of a correction, and we discuss it because its two halves belong together. Our original decision-point exhibit — a token-level competition read from a convenient measurement shortcut — was retracted when a pre-registered gate showed it disagreed with the model's true output pathway (Section 7). The post-mortem generalized: the shortcut's omissions are not random. In these bias-free architectures, the folded unembedding bias b_U — the only context-independent per-token term in the output computation — is strongly ordered by corpus frequency (Spearman ρ against log Pile counts of 0.66–0.78 across the suite, 0.779 at 12B), while the weight-geometry term the shortcut retains is frequency-dead. The convenient shortcut therefore amputates precisely the frequency channel, and its rollouts erred directionally against frequent tokens in every observed case. We bound this carefully: it is a finding about measurement instruments and about where models store their unigram prior, and it strengthens the methods contribution, not the corpus-level causal claim. But the echo is worth hearing: the frequency ordering this paper measures in the corpus is carried, at comparable or greater strength, inside the models' own output machinery — a static, context-blind thumb on every election, applied identically whether the context knows the answer or not.

Three practical implications follow, at three levels of confidence. First, established by our data: past the threshold scale, generation confidence carries no usable signal about correctness in this domain — the sign flip of Section 6 means fluency and certainty are anti-signals precisely where deployment is most likely, and tools built on these models should not surface confidence to users as trust. Second, a hypothesis our data motivates but does not establish: for specialized low-frequency domains, smaller models may outperform larger ones on applied tasks — 12B's skip link answer is categorically worse than 2.8B's — consistent with the domain-specialization literature, and testable. Third, a direction rather than a finding: the training data for this domain is not merely sparse but plausibly skewed, since the majority of web markup demonstrates inaccessible practice; the corpus may teach the anti-pattern at higher frequency than the pattern, a contamination structure our frequency counts measure only partially.

The mechanism question this paper closes at the attention level it deliberately reopens one level down: what does compute these compounds is most plausibly distributed, MLP-mediated, and not localizable by the head-level toolkit — and the late-layer binding resurgence we observe at 12B is itself a candidate attention-sink phenomenon, a testable prediction we state rather than assume. The frequency hypothesis is likewise stated to be tested, in two directions this paper's infrastructure already supports. Across domains: the compound inventory spans five professional domains, and the account predicts the same frequency-gated pattern in legal, medical, and financial knowledge. And across training time: corpus-level causality requires intervention on training data composition, which is beyond correlation's reach and this paper's scope — but checkpointed model families make the developmental version of the question tractable, and the temporal trajectory of domain knowledge across training, including whether it can be unlearned by later data, is the subject of work in progress. Accessibility was the test domain throughout; the mechanism, as far as we can measure it, is about words and their documentation density. That is precisely what makes it everyone's problem.
