## Abstract

Language models that correctly define specialized concepts routinely fail to apply them. We study this declarative-evaluative gap in digital accessibility across six Pythia scales and four GPT-2 scales. Accessibility concepts are concrete enough to code deterministically and rare enough in web text to expose frequency effects. We ask whether corpus frequency predicts which concepts emerge, which remain merely fluent, and how far those predictions reach into the model’s internal representations. The gap appears at every scale in both architectures, widening with scale in GPT-2 and closing at Pythia-12B only because declarative accuracy regresses to meet evaluative failure. We audit our own best mechanism candidate and demote it: sustained late-layer attention binding tracks emergence across domains, but fails causal necessity at three levels of ablation. What predicts the failure structure is corpus frequency: across 49 compounds, pre-registered Spearman correlations of 0.57 (Pythia) and 0.51 (GPT-2) survive confound controls, with the correct continuation present in the output distribution but outranked at the decision point. The failure is confident — the entropy penalty for wrong-domain answers shrinks toward zero with scale — and we state this consequence as a design constraint. Finally, a correction caught by our own pre-registered gate generalizes: a common normalization-skipping logit-lens variant severs the folded unembedding bias where these models store a corpus-frequency prior. Autoregressive rollouts through it fabricate trajectories at every scale tested. Four of five pre-registered claims were independently recovered in a blinded analyst study; the fifth failed as registered and produced the correction. All artifacts, criteria, and decision logs are public.
