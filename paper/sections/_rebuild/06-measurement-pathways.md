## Measurement Pathways: A Correction and What It Taught

This section documents a retraction, its cause, and the general finding the post-mortem produced. We include it as a section rather than an erratum because the mechanism behind the error is quantitative, systematic, and relevant to a common measurement practice beyond this paper. It is also the narrowed form of this paper's one claim that failed blinded validation: the pathway-disagreement claim was registered in advance as the long shot, was not recovered as stated by independent analysis, and what survived the narrowing is what follows.

**The retraction.** An earlier version of our decision-point analysis reported a token-level competition at Pythia-12B: the correct continuation of a declarative skip link prompt ranked first at 6.9B and fourth at 12B, where it appeared to lose to a specific higher-frequency competitor. The exhibit was generated by a convenient shortcut — reading the final residual stream directly against the unembedding matrix, omitting the final normalization — and it was scheduled to anchor a causal follow-up experiment. That experiment's pre-registered protocol required two gates before any intervention: reproduce the original trace under its original pathway, and confirm the true forward pass agreed at the decision step. The first gate passed. The second failed: on the model's actual output distribution, the reported election never occurs — the true winner at the decision step is a different token entirely, and the resulting generation matches the frozen behavioral record byte-for-byte. The exhibit was an artifact of its measurement pathway; no intervention was run, and the claim was withdrawn (decision log, 2026-07-04).

**The generalization.** A frozen-artifact audit then compared the shortcut's autoregressive rollouts against true-pathway generations at all six Pythia scales, using only artifacts already in version control. The rollout departs the true greedy trajectory at every scale, within seven steps, at what inspection suggests is the first thin-margin decision — after which every downstream token continues a sentence the model never produced, the rollout compounding its own error. In one case the shortcut's fiction was worse than the model's reality; in another, the model's true output was nearly correct while the shortcut reported an unrelated wrong answer. Autoregressive rollouts through this shortcut do not approximate model behavior; they fabricate it. Single-step readouts at true-pathway contexts carry a lesser and distinct caveat, and the canonical logit lens — which applies the final normalization — is not the object of this correction; the target is the normalization-skipping variant common in exploratory code, and the practice of feeding any approximate readout back into generation.

**The mechanism.** The shortcut's omissions are not neutral. Under standard weight-folding, the final LayerNorm's learned bias relocates into the unembedding bias b_U — in these architectures, which carry no explicit output bias, this folded term is the only context-independent per-token component of the logit computation, the one place a static unigram prior can live. We tested whether it does (pre-registered, prior work disclosed: the bias-frequency relationship is established for models with explicit output biases). At all six scales, b_U is strongly ordered by corpus frequency — Spearman ρ against log Pile unigram counts of 0.664–0.779, strengthening with scale and peaking at 12B — while the column-sum term the shortcut retains is frequency-dead (|ρ| < 0.01 everywhere). The shortcut therefore severs precisely the frequency channel: it evaluates every election with the model's unigram prior amputated. Consistent with this, in all six observed divergences between shortcut and truth, the true pathway elected the more frequent token and the shortcut the rarer one. Prior work documented that this measurement family is systematically biased toward some vocabulary items; the present result names the structure of that bias, its architectural location, and its cost.

**The practice.** Three rules fall out, and this paper follows all of them. Generation claims use the true forward pass, always — the trajectory is the phenomenon, and no approximation belongs inside the autoregressive loop. Vocabulary projections apply centering and the realized final-normalization scale; our direct-logit-attribution implementation did so throughout (Section 3), which is how the correction stayed localized to one exploratory trace rather than implicating the paper's projection results. And decision-point claims report margins, not just winners, because the divergences concentrate where margins are thin. The episode is also why this correction exists as a section at all: the artifact was caught before submission by a gate committed before the experiment ran, and the record of the catch — pre-registration, gates, verdict, audit, mechanism — is reproducible from the repository. We would rather the reader inherit the corrected instrument and the reason it needed correcting than either alone.

The finding also closes a loop opened in Section 4. The frequency ordering that section measures in the corpus, and shows predicting behavioral trajectories, is carried inside the model's own output computation — as a static, context-blind prior in b_U, applied identically to every election whether the context knows the answer or not. The corpus statistics and the weights tell the same story from opposite ends, and the instrument this section corrects failed precisely by cutting the wire between them.
