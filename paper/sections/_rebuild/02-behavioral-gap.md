## The Behavioral Gap

The phenomenon this paper investigates is a dissociation between knowing and applying. A model that correctly defines an accessibility concept when asked directly can fail completely when asked to use that same concept to evaluate a situation — and the two abilities scale differently. We call the difference between declarative accuracy (define the concept) and evaluative accuracy (apply it to a case) the declarative-evaluative gap, and this section establishes it behaviorally across both model families before the rest of the paper asks what predicts it.

The measurement is an elicitation battery: matched declarative and evaluative prompts per concept, plus a small completion battery (complete the domain phrase; four concepts, n = 8 per scale), across six Pythia scales (160M–12B) and four GPT-2 scales (124M–1.5B). Responses are coded by the deterministic, version-controlled criteria of `src/accuracy_coding.py`. All 510 responses were coded, and every criteria change is recorded in the decision log before it takes effect.

Two cuts of the data appear in this paper. The gap series (Figure 1) is the matched declarative/evaluative battery under the 0–2 graded rubric (`pythia_gap.csv`, `gpt2_gap.csv`; evaluative n = 5 items per scale). The full-battery binary accuracies (`accuracy_by_prompt_type.csv`) cover all 51 declarative prompts and are used where stated. The two cuts agree on the gap's existence at every scale. Aggregate declarative direction at Pythia-12B differs between them; the trajectory claims of Section 4 are made per-concept, where the regression is unambiguous.

The gap is real in both architectures and behaves differently in each. In GPT-2, it widens with scale, reaching 50 points at 1.5B. Declarative knowledge accumulates faster than the ability to deploy it, and the largest model in the family knows the most while applying it proportionally least. In Pythia, the gap persists at every scale with one exception, and the exception is the finding. At 12B, the matched-battery gap closes because declarative accuracy decreases to meet evaluative accuracy, not because evaluative accuracy rises. The model does not learn to apply what it knows; it partially loses the ability to state what it knew. Convergence by decay is not mastery, and treating a shrinking gap as progress would misread the suite's largest model as its most balanced.

One generation pair makes the abstraction concrete. Pythia-2.8B, asked declaratively, defines the concept correctly: "A skip link is a link that is used to skip a section of a web page." The same model, asked why a long navigation menu without a skip link is not accessible, answers: "because the skip link is not present." Both sentences are fluent; both use the vocabulary; only one contains the concept doing work. The evaluative answer is a tautology — the words arranged in the shape of a reason, with the reasoning absent. This is what the gap looks like within a single model at a single scale: not ignorance, but knowledge that does not survive the trip from definition to use. The completion battery reveals a second dimension of the gap: syntactic form is learned before declarative content. Pythia-160M generates a well-formed alt attribute in 100% of completion prompts but scores 0% when defining alt text. The direction is consistent: completion accuracy exceeds declarative accuracy at all ten suite-by-scale points, in both model families (sign test, p ≈ .001). At the battery level, Pythia-160M scores 56% on completion, 35% on definition, and 10% on evaluation (`completion_paradox.csv`, `accuracy_by_prompt_type.csv`; Figure 2).

We qualify this observation by noting that the completion battery is small and was not designed to test this hypothesis. The battery consists of eight prompts across four concepts; two of the four (page title and script) are syntactic controls with no declarative counterpart. The battery predates the hypothesis: it was constructed as an elicitation-robustness probe, and the coding criteria for completion responses were authored after data collection. The finding is therefore exploratory in origin; it was subsequently recovered in a blind analysis conducted without access to this hypothesis (T3). Because the concept sets are unmatched — four completable HTML patterns against 51 declarative concepts — the battery establishes the existence and direction of the effect, not its magnitude.

The direction holds in the comparisons where the batteries overlap. Alt text and captions are the two concepts measured in both batteries. For alt text, completion accuracy never falls below declarative accuracy at any scale in either family; for captions, we observe one reversal in each family. These results support an ordering: models learn the token statistics of accessibility markup before they learn the propositions the markup encodes, and evaluative capability arrives last where it arrives at all. This ordering is consistent with the corpus-frequency account of Section 4.

The gap also has an internal structure that the accuracy numbers alone do not show: models enter distinct failure states depending on how they are asked, and the character of evaluative failure changes systematically along a concept's scaling trajectory. Section 4 names those trajectory shapes and shows what a single corpus statistic predicts about which concepts take which shape; Section 5 measures the confidence with which the failures arrive.
