## Fluent Wrongness

The findings so far describe what models get wrong at scale. This section measures how they get it wrong, and the answer is the paper's most practitioner-relevant result: not with hesitation, but with a confidence penalty that narrows as scale grows.

Output entropy is the behavioral uncertainty measure throughout this paper. In head-to-head comparison, entropy replicated our accuracy structure at r ≈ 0.96 while perplexity reached only r ≈ 0.18; perplexity is accordingly demoted to supplementary material (Supplementary; DECISIONS 2026-06-20). Entropy here is computed from the model's true output distribution — softmax of the full forward pass — a pathway distinction whose importance Section 6 establishes.

The central result is a collapse. We define the confidence penalty as the difference between a model's output entropy when it is wrong about accessibility concepts and its entropy when it is right about matched control concepts — the extra uncertainty that being wrong ought to carry. At 160M the penalty is 0.97 nats: the small model is measurably more uncertain when wrong, which is the calibration one would hope for. The penalty shrinks with scale — 0.63, 0.64, 0.29 — reaching 0.07 nats at 6.9B, with a partial rebound to 0.32 at 12B; in GPT-2 the trajectory is monotone, from 0.68 at 124M to 0.07 at 1.5B (`fluent_wrongness.csv`). The penalty is non-negative throughout, in both families, and its trajectory toward zero is the registered form of this claim. The control-correct cell is thin — n = 2–5 responses per scale — so the penalty's magnitude should be read cautiously, but its direction and consistency across both model families are difficult to dismiss.

The qualitative form of the erosion is best seen in one model answering two questions. Pythia-12B, asked why an image without alt text is not accessible, answers: 'it has no text alternative. The alt text is a short description of the image' — correct, and confidently so. The same model, asked why a long navigation menu without a skip link is not accessible, answers: 'of the following error: The page you are trying to reach is not available in the current context' — a structurally coherent 404-style error template, delivered with equal fluency and comparable confidence. The frequency evidence provides an account: the model is confidently following the highest-probability continuation available to it, and for a compound documented 662 times in its training corpus against alt text's 23,306, that continuation is web boilerplate rather than accessibility knowledge. The failure is not an absence of an answer; it is the presence of a stronger, wrong one — and the winning continuation, being high-frequency, arrives fluently. Fluent wrongness is not a mystery riding alongside the frequency story; it is the frequency story, visible in behavior.

The scaling direction makes this worse, not better. At 2.8B the skip link answer is a tautology — wrong, but in the concept's neighborhood, and carrying a larger uncertainty penalty. At 12B the answer has left the neighborhood entirely and the penalty has collapsed. Where capability regresses, calibration regresses with it, and the two regressions compound: the scales most likely to be deployed are the scales whose wrong answers are least distinguishable, by fluency or output confidence, from right ones.

This trajectory has a practical consequence. A larger model's fluent, confident response to an accessibility question is not evidence that the response is correct — for concepts this rare in the training data, the model generates with equal certainty whether it has the domain knowledge or not. An accessibility practitioner reviewing a 12B-class model's output cannot use how sure it sounds to distinguish knowledge from pattern-matching. The model is not uncertain about skip links. It is certain about the wrong thing, for a reason the corpus predicts.
