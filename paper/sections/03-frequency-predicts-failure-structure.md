## Frequency Predicts Failure Structure

Plotting per-concept accuracy across scale yields four recurring shapes. Monotonic climb: accuracy rises with scale and remains high — screen reader, alt text, and WCAG follow this path in Pythia. Peak-regress: accuracy rises to a mid-scale peak and falls — skip link in Pythia is the clearest example. Never-emerges: zero correct responses at every scale in both families. ARIA, captions, and semantic HTML never arrive across ten models and two model-families; confabulation about them grows more confident as scale increases. Mixed covers the remainder. The same concept can take different trajectories in different model-families — trajectory class is a property of the concept-architecture pair, not the concept alone.

We treat this taxonomy as descriptive vocabulary, not a claim-bearing classification. A stability audit showed that shifting a single response's accuracy code by one level leaves only 20 of 102 class assignments unchanged. The taxonomy's value is as vocabulary — it lets us say "peak-regress" instead of re-describing a curve. The structure that carries empirical weight is the continuous relationship this section establishes, which operates on accuracy directly and requires no thresholded classes.

Skip link motivates the hypothesis. Its components are unremarkable English — "skip" and "link" are common words — but the compound is rare: 662 bigram occurrences in The Pile, against 32,100 for screen reader and 23,306 for alt text. Skip link is also the compound whose behavior degrades at maximum scale in both families. The hypothesis: corpus frequency of the compound predicts the trajectory.

We tested this on all 49 accessibility compounds in the elicitation battery. The battery's 51 declarative concepts yield 49 compounds for frequency analysis: ARIA and WCAG are single tokens with no defined bigram frequency, and captions is measured under its two-word form, closed captions. Compound bigram frequencies were measured with Infini-gram against The Pile and correlated against per-compound accuracy from the deterministic coding pipeline. The analysis was pre-registered with a success threshold of Spearman ρ ≥ 0.4, committed to the repository before any correlation was computed. ^4gfa0s

Both model-families clear the threshold. Pythia: ρ = 0.5715, p < 0.0001, n = 49. GPT-2: ρ = 0.5052, p = 0.0002, n = 49. The GPT-2 result carries a caveat: frequency measurements are Pile counts, and GPT-2 was trained on WebText, which is not publicly indexed. That the correlation transfers is consistent with compound-documentation density being a stable property of web text generally — but the GPT-2 result is a cross-corpus proxy, and we label it as such.

Two confounds were tested. Partialling out word1 unigram frequency leaves the Pythia partial ρ at 0.5913 and GPT-2 at 0.4857. Partialling out compound token count leaves ρ at 0.5603 and 0.4896 respectively. The association is not explained by either tested covariate, not by its pieces or its tokenization.

We attempted a within-sense analysis but could not complete it for more than 80% of compounds because there were too few correct-domain responses to analyze. This missingness is expected under the hypothesis but prevents a well-powered within-sense test. On the small subsample where analysis was possible, ρ = 0.8581 (n = 9, p = 0.003), reported as descriptive given the sample size.

At the generation step immediately following an evaluative prompt, the three illustrative cases are ordered consistently with corpus frequency. At Pythia-12B: 'alt' sits at rank 4 for the alt text prompt, 'screen' at rank 2 for screen reader — both generate correctly. 'users' sits at rank 14 for skip link, and generation collapses into an error-page pattern instead.

Tracked across scale, skip link's domain token traces the peak-regress arc in miniature: rank 12 at 2.8B, rank 7 at 6.9B, and rank 14 at 12B. From 2.8B upward it never leaves the top fifteen ranks. What changes, and what frequency predicts, is whether it wins.

Within a peak-regress trajectory, the character of failure changes across scale, not just its rate. Skip link at 2.8B fails by tautology. At 6.9B the failure is wrong but in-domain: "it is not a link." At 12B the generation has left the domain entirely, producing a fluent 404-style error template. The model does not simply get worse; it fails in progressively more confident and less recoverable ways.

Prior work establishes the macro-level relationship: pretraining document frequency predicts factual recall, term frequency predicts few-shot numerical reasoning on this same model suite, and scaling mainly improves recall of popular knowledge while the long tail lags. Our contribution is resolution and specificity: token-level rank evidence in a specialized professional domain, tied to scaling trajectories per compound, with frequency measured against the actual training corpus and accuracy deterministic and re-runnable. They describe the force; this section photographs where it lands.

Frequency predicts trajectory. The correlation survives its pre-registered threshold, its confound controls, and cross-architecture replication. Corpus-level causality is not established here — that requires intervention on training data composition. One further observation points in the same direction and is developed in Section 6: the frequency ordering measured in the corpus is echoed, at comparable or greater strength, inside the models' own output machinery.
